# Hub-and-Spoke Visual Prompt

Use this prompt with the ChatGPT image generator. The output is intended to replace the current hub-and-spoke diagram on the slide titled **“Give the workflow a return path.”**

## Primary prompt

Create a polished editorial information graphic for a senior-leadership presentation about a multi-agent decision system.

### Format and composition

- Landscape 16:9 image, 1600 × 900 pixels.
- Dark background: near-black navy, approximately `#0d1117`.
- Leave the upper 22% of the canvas mostly empty so the presentation can place a title and one sentence there.
- Leave a narrow area at the bottom clear for a short caption.
- Place the main diagram across the middle and lower portion of the canvas.
- Use generous negative space. The image should feel calm, precise and expensive rather than busy.

### Diagram

Show one central Orchestrator connected to three specialist agents:

- Market
- Competitive
- Product & Tech

The Orchestrator is the clear visual centre of gravity. It should feel like a controlled decision hub, not a robot, brain, chatbot or abstract AI symbol.

Arrange the three specialists around it in a balanced triangular composition:

- Market at upper left
- Competitive at upper right
- Product & Tech below the centre

Each specialist has a two-way connection with the Orchestrator:

- A restrained violet line travels outward from the Orchestrator, representing a scoped plan.
- A restrained green line travels inward to the Orchestrator, representing evidence and implications.

Make the two directions unmistakable through arrowheads and spacing. Avoid tangled lines. The diagram should communicate, at a glance:

1. The Orchestrator assigns distinct work.
2. Specialists return evidence.
3. The evidence meets in one place.
4. The Orchestrator can change the next assignment.

Suggest iteration with one subtle visual device, such as a partial return arc or a restrained second pulse travelling outward. Do not add a large decorative loop or make the composition resemble a network map.

### Visual language

- Editorial information design with the analytical restraint of a high-quality newspaper graphic.
- Flat vector construction with crisp geometry and disciplined alignment.
- Fine lines, subtle borders and restrained colour.
- Central Orchestrator colour: muted violet, approximately `#7b6cf6`.
- Returned evidence colour: muted green, approximately `#6bbf8a`.
- Specialist surfaces: dark charcoal, approximately `#151b23`.
- Secondary lines: blue-grey, approximately `#263040`.
- Primary text: soft white, approximately `#e8eaed`.
- Secondary text: muted grey, approximately `#8b95a5`.
- No gradients, glow effects, glassmorphism, drop shadows, neon, 3D rendering or decorative textures.

### Text

Include only these four labels, spelled exactly:

- ORCHESTRATOR
- MARKET
- COMPETITIVE
- PRODUCT & TECH

Use a clean, restrained sans-serif typeface. Do not add a title, legend, explanatory paragraph, arrow labels, numbers or any other wording. If exact typography cannot be guaranteed, omit all text while preserving clear spaces where these four labels can be overlaid later.

### Avoid

- Robots, people, faces, brains, circuitry or chatbot icons
- Generic “AI network” imagery made from dozens of glowing nodes
- SaaS dashboard cards
- Isometric or perspective views
- Corporate stock-illustration style
- Decorative complexity that does not explain the system
- Symmetry so rigid that the result feels like a logo
- Tiny text or fine detail that will disappear on a meeting-room screen

The finished image should look like a purpose-built explanation of a serious operating model, not promotional AI artwork.

## Quality check for a second generation

After generating the first version, use this follow-up if needed:

> Simplify the diagram further. Increase the negative space, strengthen the Orchestrator as the visual centre, and make the outbound violet plans and inbound green evidence readable from across a conference room. Remove anything decorative that does not explain the flow. Preserve the dark editorial style and the exact four-node topology.
