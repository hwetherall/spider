Innovera · Architecture Proposal

From a pipeline
to a network.

Our agents currently write a document. We want them to reach a decision — and to tell us honestly what risk is left standing when they do.

Harry  ·  framing ahead of the working prototype
How it works today

A relay race

Each agent completes its chapter and hands over. The baton only moves one way.

Whatever Product & Tech discovers at step 3 can never reach Market Research at step 1.
The cost

Good at coverage.
Poor at judgement.

A pipeline reliably makes sure every topic gets covered. It is a poor way to actually decide anything.

No one owns the trade-off

The call that matters sits between chapters, not inside them. Market finds demand; Competitive finds crowding. Nobody is responsible for weighing one against the other — so the tension never resolves into a decision.

Late findings land too late

Product & Tech runs last. If it surfaces something that should have reshaped the market read, that chapter is already written — and nobody goes back.

Both sides just get printed

When two agents contradict each other, the pipeline ships both statements and leaves the reader to decide who was right.

Every chapter can be individually correct and the recommendation still be wrong.
The shift

Hub and spoke

Every specialist talks to one Orchestrator. It reasons over what comes back, then sends out the next round of work.

Specialists never talk to each other directly. Their findings meet inside the Orchestrator — one place where the contradictions have to be faced.
The loop

The second dispatch is the proof

Anyone can run three agents in parallel and summarise them. What proves the system is reasoning is that round two looks different because of round one.

Round-two briefs carry their trigger: "revised because Competitive found X". That trace is what makes the reasoning auditable instead of theatrical.
The product

A decision tool.
Not a chapter printer.

The Orchestrator holds what Market, Competitive, and Product each found — names the tension between them — then sends a fresh plan back to every specialist. That loop is the product.

WHAT YOU SEE

Tension made visible

A recommendation shaped by cross-agent conflict, not three separate chapters. Residual risks called out. Round-two briefs that name their trigger — so you can see why the answer moved.

WHY THIS STACK

A loop you cannot fake in chat

Compare across agents, re-plan, dispatch into specialists that already have live tools and domain rules. That out → compare → re-plan cycle is not what a $20/month chat subscription gives you.

The value lives in the connections between agents — and in forcing the tension into the open.
The path

How we get there

The architecture is the same at every stage. Prove it small, then run it live, then widen the web.

V1 · THIS PROTOTYPE

Prove the loop

Three agents, scripted findings, real orchestration. The research is fabricated so the demo is repeatable — but the plan → report → reconcile loop genuinely runs.

V2 · LIVE

Let the agents run

Claude Managed Agents replace the fixtures behind the same schemas. A deterministic solver ranks the full option space; the model explains the trade-offs and writes the next round of plans.

V3 · SCALE

Widen the web

Extend from three agents to all ten — Revenue, Go-to-Market, Unit Economics and the rest. No redesign: each one plugs into the same hub.

Every existing agent keeps its domain, tools and skills. We are adding a layer — not rewriting one.
The ask

A pipeline produces coverage.
A network produces a decision.

The prototype that follows is deliberately scripted — the research is fabricated, the orchestration is real. What I want reviewed is the interaction architecture, not the findings.
