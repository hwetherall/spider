Innovera · Architecture Proposal

From a pipeline
to a network.

Three specialist agents can produce three correct reports and still miss the decision between them. The next step is a system that decides what work is needed next, rather than executing a fixed sequence.

A short framing before the working prototype
How it works today

A workflow with no return path

Each agent answers a fixed brief using everything that came before. Nothing it discovers can change work already completed.

The Executive Summary can reconcile the writing. It cannot reopen the research.
The cost

Good at coverage.
Poor at judgement.

A pipeline covers every topic. It does not own the trade-off between them.

Market report “Demand is strong.”
Competitive report “The segment is crowded.”
→
Executive Summary
“Demand is strong, although competition is intense.”
Still unanswered

Should we narrow the segment, test another route, or gather different evidence?

A smoother contradiction is still a contradiction.
The shift

Give the workflow a return path

The Orchestrator does not write another chapter. It holds the shared state, scopes each specialist’s work, and decides what must be investigated next.

The difference is not running agents in parallel. It is changing the next assignment when evidence changes the question.
The loop

The second dispatch is the proof

Round two is not a repeat. Each brief changes in response to what round one found.

Every revised brief names the finding that caused it to change.
Italian market entry · Round one

The obvious product
is the strategic trap.

A North American roaster wants one premium product for Italy, using its existing equipment.

Market Traditional dark-roast ground coffee — the familiar supermarket option — has the broadest demand.
Product It is the easiest option to make with existing equipment.
Competitive The category is brutal: entrenched brands already own awareness and shelf space.
→
Orchestrator conclusion The obvious answer wins on demand and feasibility — and loses on strategy.
New plans
  • Market: validate a younger urban whole-bean segment.
  • Product: configure a differentiated product on existing equipment.
  • Competitive: test whether the combined position has headroom.
The reports did not merely produce a summary. They changed what every specialist was asked to do next.
Italian market entry · Round two

A narrower, more defensible entry

The Orchestrator used the conflict to commission focused work, then reconciled the new evidence.

Recommended entry strategy

250g premium medium-roast whole-bean coffee

For younger urban Italian home-espresso consumers, positioned around traceable origin and modern Italian coffee culture.

Market Narrower, but credible demand

Urban consumers aged roughly 25–40 already make espresso from whole beans and will pay for an affordable premium ritual.

Product Still practical to make

Medium roast and whole-bean production use existing capabilities; the main addition is a premium 250g bag.

Competitive Usable headroom

The combination of modern home espresso, traceable origin and contemporary design is more defensible than traditional ground coffee.

Not the largest market. Not the emptiest whitespace. The best balance of demand, differentiation and execution.
The path

Proof now. Pilot next.

Keep the interaction model stable. Change the evidence source only after the loop earns the right to go live.

V1 · THIS PROTOTYPE

Prove the loop

Three specialists, deterministic findings, and a real plan → report → reconcile → re-plan interaction.

V2 · BOUNDED PILOT

Run one decision live

Use live specialist research on one real opportunity. Measure decision quality, traceability, and time against the current linear process.

LATER · EXPAND

Add specialists carefully

Bring in Revenue, Go-to-Market, Unit Economics, and others only after the pilot proves the decision loop adds value.

V2 is a controlled test of one claim: linked specialists produce a better, more inspectable decision.
